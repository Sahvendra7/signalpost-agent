article: Understanding the EN 148-1 Standard: Why Thread Compatibility Matters in Respiratory Protection - RSG Safety Download the Whitepaper here When selecting personal protective equipment (PPE), most attention naturally goes to filter performance and mask fit. But there is a less visible standard that plays an equally important role in day-to-day safety: EN 148-1. This standard doesn’t determine how well a filter protects against gases or particles. It determines whether a filter can physically and reliably connect to a facepiece at all. Understanding this distinction is essential for anyone responsible for procuring or specifying respiratory protective equipment. What Is EN 148-1? EN 148-1 is a European standard titled “Respiratory protective devices – Threads for facepieces – Part 1: Standard thread connection.” It specifies the standard thread dimensions used to connect filters to facepieces such as half masks and full face masks, along with the test equipment needed to assess compliance. In practice, this connection is widely known by two other names: the DIN 40mm thread or Rd40. Regardless of which term is used, all three refer to the same standardized screw connection. A Connection Standard, Not a Performance Standard This is the point that causes the most confusion, and it’s worth stating clearly: EN 148-1 says nothing about how well a filter or mask protects the wearer. It only governs whether the physical connection between filter and facepiece is standardized. Filter and mask performance is determined by separate standards: EN 143 – particle filters EN 14387 – gas and combination filters EN 136 / EN 140 – full face masks and half masks A filter can carry a full EN 148-1 thread and still offer a completely different level of protection than another filter with the same thread. When specifying respiratory protection, both standards need to be checked: the connection standard to confirm compatibility, and the relevant performance standard to confirm the protection class actually matches the risk.
